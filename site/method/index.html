Method

Method and sources

What the engine does, where every number comes from and how far to trust it.

Accuracy

Blueprint produces a factored estimate, the class used for screening and study work. Expect capital cost to fall typically within minus 30 to plus 50 percent of a detailed estimate. Mass and energy balances are tighter, as they are solved, not factored, and every design reports its own closure and atom balance error.

Process models

  • Reactors. Equilibrium reactors are solved as ideal gas chemical equilibrium by Newton iteration on the reaction extents, with equilibrium constants from Gibbs energies of formation. Adiabatic reactors are solved for the outlet temperature at which outlet enthalpy equals inlet enthalpy. Where industry runs short of equilibrium, an approach temperature is applied and stated in the inputs.
  • Separators. Vapour and liquid splits use Raoult's law over an ideal liquid, solved with the Rachford Rice equation, with Antoine vapour pressures.
  • Energy. Ideal gas enthalpy is the heat of formation at 298 K plus the integral of a fitted heat capacity, with heats of vaporisation for liquids. Steam properties use IAPWS 97 where a process model needs them.
  • Heat exchangers. Areas come from the duty, the log mean temperature difference and typical overall heat transfer coefficients (Sinnott and Towler, Chemical Engineering Design).

Thermodynamic data

Data for the 67 components come from the open source Python packages chemicals and thermo by Caleb Bell (MIT licence): critical properties, heats of formation and combustion, Gibbs energies, heat capacities, vapour pressures, flash points and flammability limits. A few condensed phase heats of formation are taken from the NIST Chemistry WebBook. GHS hazard statements are the harmonised EU CLP classifications (Regulation 1272/2008, Annex VI); always use the supplier safety data sheet.

Generated from the open source Python packages chemicals and thermo (Caleb Bell, MIT licence). Ideal gas Cp fitted as a cubic in T over 298 to 1200 K.

Capital cost

Purchased equipment cost follows Turton et al., Analysis, Synthesis and Design of Chemical Processes, 5th edition (2018), Appendix A: log10Cp0 = K1 + K2 log10A + K3 (log10A)2. Bare module cost applies the pressure and material factors, and grassroots fixed capital is 1.18 times the sum of bare module costs plus 0.50 times the bare module cost at base conditions. Units above the correlation range are split into identical parallel trains rather than extrapolated. Costs are escalated with the Chemical Engineering Plant Cost Index from the Turton base (CEPCI 397, 2001) to 2024 (CEPCI 800).

Operating cost and returns

Cost of manufacture follows Turton Eq. 8.2 without depreciation: 0.18 FCI + 2.73 COL + 1.23 (CUT + CWT + CRM). Operating labour uses the Alkhayat and Gerrard correlation (Turton Eq. 8.3) and the US BLS mean wage for chemical plant operators. The plant runs 8,000 hours a year. NPV, IRR and payback come from a discounted cash flow over the plant life with the tax rate and discount rate stated in each design.

Carbon

Scope 1 covers fuel combustion, process CO2, N2O and methane slip. Scope 2 covers grid electricity and net steam, at the regional grid factor. Upstream feedstock emissions are not included. Emission factors are listed with their sources below.

Validation

Each design is compared with published figures for real plants: energy use per tonne, conversion, emissions, capital and production cost, taken from industry best available technique documents, IPCC guidance, licensor papers and peer reviewed studies. Each check has a tolerance that reflects how precise the published figure is, and the result is shown on the design page as a pass or a fail. Benchmarks on file:
